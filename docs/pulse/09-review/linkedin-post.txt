The Self-Improving Products Wave

Have your product on autopilot?

The market is taking shape around three starting points:

→ PostHog and Amplitude: understand what users need.
→ Sentry and Datadog: understand what broke in production.
→ Anthropic, OpenAI and GitHub: give agents the tools to act.

Then come the specialists: Warp, Cursor and Cognition for execution; Veris and Antithesis for verification; Greptile, CodeRabbit and Renovate for recurring maintenance.

For Autopilot Pulse #09, I mapped 20 companies across five categories and ten subcategories, then examined seven implementation reports from 14 July–14 September.

Three cases stood out:

• PostHog traced product friction to a fix with a public PR, human approval and recorded production deployments on 29 July.

• Sentry rolled out an internal Seer + Claude workflow in mid-July. It generates fixes, finds reviewers and collects feedback. Review ownership is part of the system.

• loveholidays reported more than ten new search experiences built with its Codex-powered Playground, with at least three live on its website. People still choose the work and define the checks.

The interesting shift is the connection between finding a problem, preparing a change and learning from what happens next.

The newsletter includes the market map, source links and a clear distinction between live deployments, internal use and demonstrations.

What would you put on autopilot first: finding product friction, fixing bugs, checking changes or maintaining dependencies?
