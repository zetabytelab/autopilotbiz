Autopilot Pulse #10 is out: The harness tax on self-driving products.

PostHog runs over 90 scouts on its own product. One loop: a user complains, a scout files a report five minutes later, a PR is open eight minutes after that, and it is in production the same working day. They publish a price too — $15 per merged PR.

Shopify's River works the other end. A vulnerability entry triggers it, and in the first eleven days the backlog fell about 70%. The part worth stealing is not the agent, it is the gate: CI computed against a stale head gets rejected. That is what makes the loop affordable.

Then a Berkeley study, HarnessTax, holds the model constant and varies the scaffolding. Claude Fable 5 on SWE-bench Lite: 97.8% in Claude Code, 96.7% in Codex, 96.7% in Pi — at $1.33, $0.89 and $0.67 per attempt. Near-identical accuracy. Up to 5x apart on cost elsewhere in the table.

So cost per durable improvement has four levers, not one: harness, model, tools, task.

And the number none of them publishes: what fraction of agent-authored changes had to be reverted.
